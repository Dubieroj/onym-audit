I.

Anatomy of an attestation

One auditor’s visible opinion — never a licence, a gate, or a substitute for your own trust decision. Every field below is mandatory, and a client that cannot render one must not show the attestation at all.

  1. A name you can hold to account. The auditor signs with a key its manifest ties to a person, a contact, and published liability terms.
  2. Bytes, not brands. The digest of the component’s signed manifest — and of the one served document the result depends on. Change a byte and the attestation stops applying.
  3. A named, versioned method. A result class is only as good as the suite behind it; the suite is published and re-runnable.
  4. Scope is the whole claim. What was not examined travels with what was. A reader of the result alone must not be more reassured than a reader of the scope.
  5. The money is visible. Who paid, and every relationship with the subject, sits in the signed object — not a footnote.
  6. Opinions expire. Judgment ages; a deployment examination lasts 30 days, then says nothing.
  7. The subject answers back. Its signed reply is served beside the verdict, and the auditor cannot edit it.

II.

Who uses it, and how

Four people, one object. The attestation is the same signed file in every story; what changes is who reads it and what they decide.

A person using Onym

Choosing where her backups live

  1. Settings → Backup operators lists three operators her Discovery catalogs found.
  2. Her app fetches their attestations and checks each one against the operator’s exact signed manifest, the auditor’s key, and a fresh status list.
  3. One reads conformance-run · clear · 12 days ago · paid by the operator (declared). One is unexamined. The third was examined, but its manifest changed last night — so the old verdict no longer shows.

She decides with evidence, and nothing is blocked. Shipping clients don’t read attestations yet — Discovery’s evidence field is deferred to its v2, and this profile is the candidate shape. Today: this page and the CLI.

An operator

Proving a new courier is conformant

  1. He runs a relay and publishes its signed manifest.
  2. He sends a signed order. The fee is the same whatever the result — no other kind can be written.
  3. The suite runs; findings reach him first; he fixes them; the re-run is clear, bound to his manifest’s digest. He staples the status list beside his manifest.

Users and catalogs get a claim they can check, instead of “trust me”. If he ships new bytes, he needs a new examination.

A catalog curator

Curating without becoming a gatekeeper

  1. Her Discovery policy says: couriers with a current conformance-run, at most 30 days old, from auditors on this list.
  2. The attestations are portable: a rival catalog can weigh the same ones differently, and users can still import any courier directly.
  3. When a verdict is revoked or expires, her next snapshot follows — on evidence anyone can re-check.

Her curation becomes auditable, instead of a private opinion with a logo.

The first examination ● live

Onym’s own Discovery catalog

  1. On 23 September the 42-check suite ran against discovery.onym.app, from public bytes only.
  2. Findings go to the maintainer first. Under the unsolicited-examination policy the attestation is published no sooner than 12 hours after notice.
  3. Onym can answer with a signed reply shown beside it. When the catalog is republished, the attestation stops matching and a re-run supersedes it.

The whole lifecycle, on a real component, this week. The attestation is published — it is in the register below: verify it yourself.

IV.

Five rules this auditor cannot break

Not promises — properties of the documents and the code. Each one is checked by the published fixtures.

  1. Bytes, not brands

    An attestation names a git commit, a build hash, or a deployment’s signed manifest digest. A fixed deployment never inherits a fail; a broken one never inherits a clear.

  2. The seal stays at home

    The auditor’s key signs attestations offline. The server holds only a delegated status key: it can refresh freshness every six hours, but cannot mint, alter, or revoke a single verdict.

  3. Checking status reveals nothing

    One signed list covers every attestation. There is no per-attestation lookup, so asking “is this still valid?” never tells the auditor what you are about to rely on.

  4. Verdicts are not for sale

    The only fee models that parse are pro-bono and fixed-verdict-independent. A bonus for a clean result is not forbidden by policy — it is unexpressible.

  5. Absence is absence

    An unexamined component shows as unexamined, never as failed. Which auditors count is the reader’s choice; nothing here is a gate to using anything.

V.

From examination to expiry

  1. ExamineA published suite runs black-box against public bytes.
  2. NotifyFindings go to the subject first. Exploitable ones are embargoed up to 90 days.
  3. PublishA signed attestation, bound to the exact bytes examined.
  4. ReplyThe subject posts a signed answer; it appears beside the verdict.
  5. Supersede · revokeA re-run replaces it; a mistake withdraws it, with a reason.
  6. ExpireThirty days on, the opinion says nothing more.

VII.

Commission an examination

For operators

Run a courier, a catalog, a notary? Send a signed AuditOrder — subject and sponsor sign, the auditor countersigns only after checking its independence policy.

POST https://foldy.io/audit/orders

Order a review

For subjects

Examined without asking? Unsolicited examinations cover public bytes only, and you are notified before publication. Reply with a signed SubjectResponse; it is published beside the attestation, unedited.

POST https://foldy.io/audit/responses

For everyone

Payment buys examination effort under a published method. It cannot buy a result class, an early exploit, silence, or display priority.
